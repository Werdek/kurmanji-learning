## 2. B1 Exit Targets

The B1 exit standard is aligned to the Council of Europe’s CEFR B1 descriptors and the CEFR Companion Volume (2020). It is an original course examination, not a replica of a specific national board.

| Skill | B1 exit target | Course evidence | Minimum exit standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reading** | Understand the main points of clear standard texts on familiar topics; follow short narratives; scan for specific information; understand descriptions of events, feelings, and wishes | 60+ graded texts; 18 stage reading tasks; 6 exam reading sets | ≥70% on the B1 reading section |
| **Spoken interaction** | Deal with most situations likely while travelling in Kurdistan; enter unprepared conversation on familiar topics; express and justify opinions | 54 speaking tasks; 18 role-plays; 6 discussion tasks | ≥60% on the speaking rubric |
| **Spoken production** | Connect phrases to describe experiences, dreams, hopes, and ambitions; give brief reasons and explanations | 18 monologue tasks; 6 presentation tasks | ≥60% on the speaking rubric |
| **Written production** | Write simple connected text on familiar topics; write personal emails/letters describing experiences and impressions | 36 guided writing tasks; 6 exam writing sets | ≥60% on the writing rubric |
| **Language use** | Reasonable control of frequent structures; sufficient vocabulary for familiar topics; errors rarely impede communication | 41 core grammar rules; 1,900 lexical items; 108 lessons | ≥50% on language use; ≥70% overall mastery |
| **Orthography** | Accurate spelling of core vocabulary with ç, ê, î, ş, û; consistent Hawar conventions | Typed exercises and guided writing from A1.1 onward | ≥90% orthography accuracy on A1–A2 core items |
| **Sociocultural competence** | Use polite forms; understand hospitality, invitations, Newroz, and common social routines | U12, U18; authentic texts; culture notes | Pass on checkpoint tasks |

Listening comprehension is **not assessed** in this course. Learners who need a listening qualification should practise with external Kurmanji media and seek a separate listening assessment.

### B1 can-do checklist

A learner who passes the exit examination can:

- Introduce themselves and others; ask and answer questions about home, family, work, and interests.
- Handle travel, shopping, food, health, and everyday service encounters.
- Narrate a past event and distinguish what happened from what was happening.
- Describe plans, hopes, and ambitions; give reasons and simple explanations.
- Read the main idea and key details of a short news item, interview, or story.
- Write a personal email and a short opinion text with connected sentences.
- Use the main case, tense, and clause patterns with enough control to be understood.
- Recognise common dialect variants in writing without being derailed by them.

## 3. Course Architecture

### 3.1 Unit structure

Every unit contains six lessons:

1. **Context and input** — a dialogue or short text introduces the theme and the target language in use.
2. **Grammar discovery** — the learner notices a pattern, then gets a concise rule and examples.
3. **Controlled practice** — fill-in-the-blank, multiple choice, matching, and sentence building.
4. **Reading and text work** — a short text, dialogue, notice, or menu with comprehension and spelling work.
5. **Integrated task** — a real-world role-play, writing task, or presentation.
6. **Retrieval and mastery** — mixed review, interleaved old items, and checkpoint preparation.

A1 and A2 lessons contain **6–7 exercise items** and are designed for **15–30 minutes**. B1 lessons may grow to **8–12 items** and **25–35 minutes**. A learner may stop after any item; progress is saved locally.

**Counting rules**

- A lexical item is a headword, a fixed expression, or a core function word.
- Proper nouns (countries, cities, people) are counted only when they are taught as vocabulary, not when they appear incidentally.
- Regular ordinal forms are not counted separately from their cardinal stems.
- Dialect variants are not double-counted; one form is productive and the other is tagged receptive.
- Compound verbs are counted as one item where the meaning is lexicalised (`dest pê kirin`, `hezkirin`), and as two items where the parts are transparently compositional.

### 7.3 Grammar sequencing rationale

- **A1.1** teaches high-frequency chunks before full analysis (`Navê min … e`) and delays the transitive past until the learner has a stable present tense and pronoun system.
- **A1.2** expands the noun phrase and spatial language; the oblique is introduced through definite objects and prepositions, not as an abstract table.
- **A2.1** introduces the past in two steps: intransitive first, then the ergative transitive. This prevents the common error of treating `min` as a subject pronoun.
- **A2.2** moves from single clauses to clause combining, but only with high-frequency subordinators.
- **B1.1** consolidates narrative and public language: perfect tenses, passive, relative clauses, conditionals.
- **B1.2** focuses on register, argumentation, authentic texts, and exam performance rather than new core morphology.

### 7.4 High-risk error clusters to teach explicitly

| Error cluster | Example of the error | Correct target | Where addressed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Using `ez` as the agent of a past transitive | `*Ez nan xwar.` | `Min nan xwar.` | U8 |
| Forgetting patient agreement | `*Min ew dîtin.` for “I saw him” | `Min ew dît.` | U8 |
| Using postconsonantal copula after a vowel | `*Ez mamoste im.` | `Ez mamoste me.` | U1, U3 |
| Treating `wê` as a universal future particle | `*Ez wê biçim.` | `Ez dê biçim.` / `Ewê biçe.` | U11 |
| Overusing `hatin` passive for every verb | `*Ew hate çûn.` | `Ew çû.` | U14 |
| Using `ji` without the correct oblique complement | `*ji tu re` | `ji te re` | U3, U12 |
| Mixing ezafe and oblique after prepositions | `*li ser mase` | `li ser maseyê` | U5, U9 |
| Dropping the plural agreement in ergative past | `*Min du kitêb xwend.` | `Min du kitêb xwendin.` | U8 |
| Using `ku` without the correct antecedent state | `*Pirtûk ku min xwend` | `Pirtûka ku min xwend` | U12, U14 |
| Translating “I have” with a verb “to have” | `*Ez kitêb heme` | `Min kitêb heye.` | U2 |

## 8. B1 Assessment Blueprint

### 8.1 Examination structure (text-first)

| Section | Time | Points | Tasks | Construct |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Reading | 45 min | 40 | 4 tasks: matching headings, multiple choice, true/false/not given, short answer | Main idea, detail, inference, vocabulary in context |
| Language use | 30 min | 30 | 50 items: 30 grammar, 20 vocabulary | Case, tense, agreement, subordination, collocation |
| Writing | 35 min | 15 | 2 tasks: informal email (80–100 words); opinion text (120–150 words) | Task achievement, coherence, range, accuracy, orthography |
| Speaking | 12 min | 15 | 4 tasks: interview, picture description, role-play, discussion | Fluency, pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar, interaction |
| **Total** | **110 min written + 12 min speaking = 122 min** | **100** | | |

There is no listening section. Listening is recommended as optional external practice, not as part of the assessed course.

### 8.2 Pass criteria

- **Overall:** ≥60/100.
- **Section minimum:** no section below 50%.
- **Writing:** ≥9/15 (60%).
- **Speaking:** ≥9/15 (60%).
- **Distinction:** ≥80/100.
- **Retakes:** unlimited; each attempt draws a different form from the item bank where possible.

### 8.3 Writing rubric (0–3 per criterion; 15 total)

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Task achievement | Off task / too short | Partly addresses task | Addresses task adequately | Fully addresses task with relevant detail |
| Coherence and cohesion | No organisation | Basic links, weak paragraphing | Clear organisation, adequate connectors | Well organised, natural connectors |
| Lexical range | Insufficient | Basic repetition | Adequate range for B1 | Good range, some less common items |
| Grammatical accuracy | Errors block meaning | Frequent errors, meaning often unclear | Meaning clear; noticeable errors | Good control; errors do not impede |
| Orthography | Unintelligible spelling | Frequent `ê/î/û/ş/ç` errors | Mostly accurate | Accurate core spelling |

### 8.4 Speaking rubric (0–3 per criterion; 15 total)

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fluency | Cannot sustain | Frequent long pauses | Some hesitation but sustains | Fluent enough for B1 |
| Pronunciation | Unintelligible | Difficult to follow | Intelligible with L1 influence | Clear, consistent stress and vowels |
| Vocabulary | Insufficient | Basic words only | Adequate for familiar topics | Good range and circumlocution |
| Grammar | No control | Fragmented | Reasonable control of frequent structures | Good control; complex attempts |
| Interaction | No response | Minimal response | Responds and asks simple questions | Initiates, negotiates meaning, develops |

### 8.5 Item bank and forms

- Minimum **3 equivalent mock forms**.
- Minimum **3× exam length** in the item bank for each section.
- Every item tagged by stage, unit, grammar rule, lexeme, skill, and difficulty.
- No form published until all items pass source checking and bias review.
- Writing and speaking prompts are reviewed for accessibility and cultural appropriateness.

### 8.6 Readiness calculation

```
Readiness = 0.40 × SRS_mastery
          + 0.30 × mock_score
          + 0.20 × checkpoint_average
          + 0.10 × can_do_coverage
```

- `SRS_mastery`: proportion of A1–B1 core items at mastery level ≥3, weighted by stage.
- `mock_score`: best valid mock score in the last 30 days, capped at 100.
- `checkpoint_average`: mean of the six stage checkpoint scores.
- `can_do_coverage`: learner self-rating plus evidence from completed tasks.

“Ready” requires ≥75% readiness, mock ≥60, and writing/speaking ≥60%. The learner can still practise or retake anything.

## 9. Mastery and Progression

### 9.1 Mastery levels

| Level | Label | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Unseen | No attempt |
| 1 | Introduced | Seen in a lesson or review |
| 2 | Recognized | Correct recognition with support |
| 3 | Recalled | Correct production without hints |
| 4 | Independent | Correct in at least two domains, spaced ≥3 days |
| 5 | Durable | Production + grammar + recognition, with a success after ≥7 days |

The schema retains a `"listening"` review domain for stored-data compatibility, but no exercise emits it and the Progress dashboard does not display it.

### 9.2 Stage readiness

- A unit is **secure** when ≥80% of its core items are at level ≥3.
- A stage is **ready** when ≥70% of its items are at level ≥3 and the stage checkpoint is passed.
- A stage is **strong** when ≥85% of items are at level ≥3 and the checkpoint is ≥85%.
- These labels affect recommendations, not access.

### 9.3 No artificial barriers

- No hearts, lives, energy, or currency.
- No daily practice cap.
- No “wait until tomorrow” state.
- No content locked behind a streak.
- A learner can open any unit, any checkpoint, and any mock form at any time.
- XP and streaks are cosmetic and optional.

## 10. Phonology and Orthography Scope

### 10.1 Alphabet

The Hawar/Bedirxan Latin alphabet has 31 letters:

`a b c ç d e ê f g h i î j k l m n o p q r s ş t u û v w x y z`

Key values for English-speaking learners:

| Letter | Approximation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `ç` | `ch` in “church” | Distinct from `c` |
| `c` | `j` in “jam” | Voiced affricate |
| `ş` | `sh` in “ship” | Distinct from `s` |
| `x` | Scottish `ch` in “loch” | Voiceless uvular fricative |
| `q` | uvular stop | Not the same as English `k` |
| `ê` | long vowel, roughly “ay” without glide | Meaning-bearing length |
| `î` | long `ee` | Meaning-bearing length |
| `û` | long `oo` | Meaning-bearing length |
| `j` | `zh` in “measure” | Not English `j` |

### 10.2 Vowels and stress

- Vowels: `a, e, ê, i, î, o, u, û`.
- Length is phonemic for `ê`, `î`, `û`; do not reduce them to `e`, `i`, `u`.
- Stress is generally final, with lexical exceptions. Written descriptions and external audio are recommended for stress practice; the app does not assess pronunciation.
- Aspirated vs unaspirated voiceless stops are contrastive in Kurmanji phonology (Thackston, Phonology). The course notes the distinction but does not drill it as a listening skill.

### 10.3 Orthographic policy

- Productive content uses Hawar/Bedirxan orthography only.
- Arabic and Cyrillic script appear only in a labelled “script awareness” note at B1, never as the target form.
- The course does not “simplify” `ê/î/û/ş/ç` to ASCII. The Kurdish keyboard and character palette make correct input easy.
- Common spelling variants are recorded in the lexeme record and accepted only when source-verified.
- Month names vary by region; one core set is taught, alternatives are tagged receptive.

### 10.4 Dialect policy

- Productive target: Standard Literary Kurmanji with an Amed/Diyarbakır reference variety.
- Receptive exposure: Qamishli/Syrian and Serhed variants at A2–B1, clearly labelled.
- Badini forms may appear as receptive glosses at B1 but are not the productive model.
- Variants such as `hûn/hun`, `şem/şemî`, `rûnê/rûne`, `biçim/herim`, `bêm/werim` are recorded rather than treated as errors.
- No pronunciation or voice baseline is enforced because the course has no content-audio component.

## 11. Content QA and Source Policy

### 11.1 Every lesson must carry

- `sourceRefs` for each grammar point and each lexeme introduced.
- `reviewStatus` for each Kurdish string: `draft`, `checked`, `native-reviewed`, or `verified`. A1 and A2 publish at `checked`.
- An explanation for every answer key, including why distractors are wrong.
- A dialect tag where a form is not standard literary.
- Review targets that resolve to the unit's lexeme and grammar refs.

### 11.2 Prohibited practices

- Generating Kurdish by recursive machine translation.
- Using Google Translate, Glosbe, Tatoeba, or unverified social-media text as a primary source.
- Publishing an example sentence that has not been checked against a grammar or dictionary source.
- Inventing a dialect form to fill a vocabulary gap.
- Silently “correcting” a source without logging the conflict.
- Adding an audio, listening, or microphone-dependent exercise to the core course.

### 11.3 Review workflow

1. Author drafts the English prompt and the Kurdish target.
2. Source check against Thackston/Rizgar/Bedirxan/Chyet.
3. Schema and answer-key validation.
4. Cross-reference check: lexemes, grammar refs, review targets, duplicate IDs.
5. Publication at `reviewStatus: "checked"` when steps 1–4 pass.
6. Optional later review can raise a string to `native-reviewed` or `verified`, but it is not a publication gate.

### 11.4 Golden set

A curated set of 200–300 checked sentences and conjugation tables is maintained as a regression suite. Any content change that alters tokenisation, answer keys, or explanations must pass the golden set.

## 12. Scope Boundaries

- **Levels:** A1.1–B1.2 only. No B2/C1 content in the core path.
- **Varieties:** Standard Literary Kurmanji productive; Sorani, Zazaki, and Gorani are out of scope.
- **Scripts:** Hawar Latin productive; Arabic/Cyrillic only as labelled awareness notes.
- **Certification:** The B1 examination is an original CEFR-aligned readiness assessment, not an accredited certificate.
- **Speaking:** Self/tutor rubric, not automatic ASR grading.
- **Authentic texts:** Public domain or licensed; adaptations logged.
- **Modality:** Text-first. No content audio input, listening exercises, microphone capture, or TTS in the assessed course. Optional interface tones are UI-only and not assessed.
